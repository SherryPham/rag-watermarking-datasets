Mrs Sankey's List

Deborah Sankey runs the kitchen at Wrackley Street Primary and has a list of six children taped inside a cupboard door. She has had a list since the first week of the scheme in 2027, and she takes it very seriously in fact.

The programme is simple to describe. Between ten past and half past nine every weekday, every adult in the building listens to one child read. Wrackley Street has 214 pupils and 31 adults, and the second number includes the bureau, the caretaker and the kitchen.

When Helena Mboya, who became head in 2025, first proposed this, the kitchen was one of the objections raised on Sankey's behalf rather than by her. Lunch for 214 people starts early, and twenty minutes out of the middle of the morning was assumed to be impossible. Sankey's own position, which she says nobody queried for at first, was that she could move the twenty minutes if somebody informed her which twenty minutes mattered.

She now reads with six children on a rota and has, by her own uneven count, heard something over four thousand individual sessions. She is regarded within the school as one of the two or three most effective adults at it, a fact that Mboya cites whenever she's enquired why the scheme cannot simply be run by teachers.

Sankey attributes this to the four-hour training and the one-page script, which she keeps in the same cupboard. Her account of what the script trained her is that she had assumed the job was to help, and that the training's main work was to stop her doing so. A youngster who has stopped on a word is working, and the adult's task is to wait longer than feels comfortable.

The record cards are kept by hand in a box in the corridor. Each youngster has one, showing who heard them read and when. Sankey fills hers in after lunch service. The cards are the only administrative trace the programme produces and the school has resisted several suggestions to computerise them, on the grounds that the box is visible and a database would not be.

In the four years to 2031 the proportion of pupils reading at age level went from 51 percent to 84 percent. The authority's evaluation in 2032 found the largest gains among those who had begun furthest behind, and Sankey's list is weighted toward exactly that group, as are most of the lists.

Twelve schools in the authority have adopted the model. Sankey has been enquired to speak at two of them and didn't enjoy it, but she went, because in both cases the question being argued over was whether non-teaching staff could really do this.

Her reply both times was that she hadn't thought she could either, and that the difference between her and a tutor in this particular twenty minutes is smaller than everybody assumes. She has said the children worked it out long before the adults did, and that none of hers has ever once treated reading with the kitchen supervisor as a lesser version of the thing.
