March 1994

The last keeper left Ruane Head in March 1994, and for thirty-five years the station was unmanned. The cottages were traded in 1997.

Automation was, at the time, widely accepted. The economics were plain, the technology was adequate, and the keepers themselves had in many cases been arguing for years that the post had become one of watching equipment that watched itself.

Ruane Head's tower is 14 metres on a headland, its focal plane 46 metres above the sea, and it covers an approach used by about 300 fishing ships a year. The light has never failed. That record is real and it is the reason the automation of the district has generally been judged a success.

The haze signal is the qualification, and it took thirty-five years to surface.

In 2029 the engineer Teodora Balan established that the signal had not been sounding for at least four years, and that the monitoring system had relayed it as working all through. The sensor confirmed that the command had been issued, not that any sound had emerged.

Nobody had spotted since nobody was there. A keeper in 1985 would have been aware within one shift, not through diligence but because a fog signal is extremely loud and its absence in fog is impossible to miss from a hundred metres away.

This is the thing the district has had to absorb. The automated station did not fail to monitor its fog signal through negligence. It monitored it precisely as specified, and the specification had been written by people who had never stood on the headland in fog, because standing on the headland in mist was not any more part of anybody's job.

Acoustic confirmation — a microphone that listens and recounts what it hears — was retrofitted to all 31 stations in the district by 2032, at a cost of 2.4 million. Two others had the same fault.

Ruane Head now has a resident warden, which it had not had since 1994. The post is jointly financed with a seabird trust that monitors 4,000 pairs on the cliffs below the light, and it spans both. The cottages sold in 1997 were bought back in 2033 to house the post.

The authority is careful about what this does and does not represent. Thirty-one stations are not getting wardens. The setup at Ruane Head works because a second organisation desired somebody on that headland anyway, and the two half-requirements happened to add up to one post.

No vessel was lost in the four years the signal was silent. Local opinion in the fishing community treats that as a near miss instead of a vindication, and the point most frequently made isn't about instrumentation at all. It is that for four years, skippers coming in on a thick night had been counting on something that was not there, and had no way of discovering out.

Skippers out of the nearest port have since taken to reporting whether they heard the signal on the way in, which the authority did not ask for and now collects. It duplicates the microphones exactly, and nobody involved has suggested discontinuing it.
