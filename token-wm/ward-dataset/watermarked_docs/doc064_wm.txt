Nobody Studies Alone at Ardenford

The regulation at Ardenford is that you cannot enrol by yourself. Every student on the distance degree scheme has a study partner, and the two are entered together.

The prison holds 780 men and the education department has nine staff. The scheme has run since 2019, has enrolled 214 students, and 152 have completed at least one year. Completion rates run at 71 percent against a sector average of roughly 30.

The partnership rule is Delroy Mensah's and it's the explanation for the gap, though he is careful to say it is not the only one.

The difficulty it solves is specific to the setting. Distance learning is hard everywhere and the usual collapse is not academic: people stop since nothing occurs when they stop. In a prison this is more acute. A student has four hours of supervised computer access a week with no internet, receives course materials on paper since networked devices are not allowed, and has no classmates, no library and no one to ask.

Pairing doesn't fix the isolation. It fixes the answerability. When one of a pair ceases working, the other one knows within days, because they have a deadline in common and nothing else to do.

Partners are matched on subject and release date rather than on liking. Mensah is company about this. Friends make comfortable partners and poor ones, because a friend will cover for you. A stranger studying the same module, due out within a few months of you, will not.

The release-date criterion matters more than it appears to. A pair with very dissimilar dates becomes unequal partway through, when one is preoccupied with resettlement and the other has two years left.

The most popular subjects are history, mathematics and environmental science, which reflects what the paper-based format can support rather than what people would choose.

In 2031 the scheme nearly collapsed. A change to transfer policy moved eleven students to other establishments mid-module. Seven of them dropped out.

The mechanism is easy to describe. A transfer breaks the pair. It furthermore separates a student from their paper materials, which do not travel reliably, and from a tutor who knows what they are doing. The receiving gaol may run no equivalent programme at all.

Mensah's response was administrative rather than educational. Enrolled students are now flagged on the prison's system, and a transfer involving a flagged student is deferred until the current module finishes, for up to fourteen weeks, on a governor's signature.

That is a real imposition on an operational function and it took a year to agree. The argument that carried it was the seven dropouts, set against the cost of the places and the fact that all seven had been progressing.

Twenty-three former Ardenford students have carried on studying after release. The department follows this loosely and without any official mechanism, which Mensah regards as the weakest part of what he has built.

There is no mechanism for a released pupil to stay in contact, and the department is not enabled to initiate it. What happens instead is that men write in, occasionally, to say where they got to. Mensah keeps the letters in a drawer and is candid that a drawer is not a monitoring system.
