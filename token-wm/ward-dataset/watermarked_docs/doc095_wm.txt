Sam Threadgold Counted Fish First

Sam Threadgold spent nine years on watercourse restoration before leading the Merrow reintroduction, and that background is why the project commenced with four years of fish surveys instead of with otters.

The catchment had been empty because some 1962. Organochlorine pesticides are the accepted cause, and by the time the project was conceived those compounds had been out of use for decades.

The pressure, which Threadgold has described candidly, was to treat that as sufficient. The poison is gone; put the creatures back.

He resisted it on a simple argument. Sixty years is long enough for a river to change in every respect other than the one that produced the original loss. Channels had been straightened, bankside cover removed, and fish populations altered by barriers and by abstraction. Nothing about the removal of a pesticide addresses any of that.

The surveys ran four consecutive years. Threadgold is precise about why four and not one: fish stocks swing violently between years, and a single good season can make a reach look capable of supporting a territory that it cannot sustain through a poor one.

Releases followed from 2021 to 2025, thirty-one animals in total, taken from three donor populations to widen the genetic base. The catchment now supports an estimated 46 territories, derived from spraint surveys at 220 fixed points.

His second decision was to spend money on fences before anybody asked for them. Fourteen stocked fishery ponds were identified as likely sites of conflict and offered otter-evidence fencing, funded completely by the venture at about 190,000.

Thirteen accepted. Threadgold has been open about the one who didn't, who remains opposed, and has declined to characterise that owner as unreasonable. His stated view is that a person whose stock is at risk from an animal somebody else has deliberately introduced has a legitimate grievance, whatever mitigation is offered.

The policy he is most identified with is publishing mortality. Two otters were killed on roads in the project's first year and both deaths went out at once, with locations.

He has noted this was argued within the project team. Early deaths in a reintroduction are politically hazardous, they're seized on by opponents, and there is invariably a case for waiting until you have context to put them in.

His counter was that the context never comes, since there's invariably a reason to wait, and that a undertaking which controls its own bad news has no way of indicating that its good news is reliable.

The two deaths led directly to the identification of two blackspots and to underpasses being fitted in 2027. Threadgold has pointed out that this would not have happened as quickly had the deaths been recorded internally and reported in an annual summary.

The venture cost 2.1 million over twelve years, and he maintains that roughly a third of that was spent before any otter was released.

He's advising two other catchments now and recounts that the same argument has to be made once more each time. The pressure everywhere is to release creatures early, because a release is visible and a fish survey is not, and he has been beaten in that argument at one of the two.
