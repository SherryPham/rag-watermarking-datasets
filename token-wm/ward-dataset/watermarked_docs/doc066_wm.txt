The Eleven Who Were Moved

In the spring of 2031 eleven men studying for degrees at Ardenford were transferred to other prisons in the midpoint of a module. Seven of them never finished the course.

The transfers were routine. None of the eleven was relocated for anything to do with education; the reasons were the reasons transfers invariably happen, which are population management, security assessment and proximity to family, and each individual decision was defensible.

The upshot on the scheme was nearly terminal.

Ardenford has run a distance degree scheme because 2019 for a population of 780 men, staffed by nine people in the education department. Its distinguishing feature is that nobody enrols alone. Students work in pairs, matched on subject and release date, and the pairing is what causes a completion rate of 71 percent against a sector average of roughly 30.

A transfer severs the pair. It additionally does two other things that are less obvious from outside. It separates a student from paper course substances, which the prison employs since no networked devices are permitted and which do not follow a prisoner reliably between establishments. And it delivers the student to a prison that may run no equivalent programme, where they are an individual distance learner with no partner, no teacher who knows them, and maybe no books.

Seven of the eleven stopped. The remaining four carried on, and three of those four went to the same receiving prison, which happened to have a comparable program.

What made the loss conspicuous was that all eleven had been doing well. This was not attrition amongst strugglers. These were men who had completed assignments on schedule and were, in several cases, doing better than they had at any point in formal education.

Delroy Mensah, who constructed the programme, spent the following year arguing for an administrative change, and got it. Enrolled students are flagged on the prison arrangement. A transfer involving a flagged student is deferred until the current module is complete, up to a maximum of fourteen weeks, and the deferral requires a governor's signature.

The signature requirement is not a rubber stamp. It means someone senior is accountable for smallholding a transfer, and it means the education department cannot simply block movement at will.

The policy has been in place because 2032. Nobody at Ardenford claims it's a complete answer. Fourteen weeks is shorter than some modules, the flag depends on the department retaining its records current, and a governor can decline.

What it has done is make the cost visible. Before 2031 the scheme's exposure to transfer was real and had never been counted, because students who disappeared into the system were simply recorded as having withdrawn.

Of 214 students enrolled since 2019, 152 have completed no less than a year, and 23 have carried on studying after release. The seven who stopped in 2031 are still, by the department's own reckoning, the most instructive thing that has happened to the programme.

Two of the seven have since resumed their studies at other establishments, which the department knows only because both men penned to say so. The other five are, thus far as anybody at Ardenford is aware, not any more studying anywhere.
