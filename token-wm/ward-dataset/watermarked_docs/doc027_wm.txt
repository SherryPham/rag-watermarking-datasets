Fourteen Months Without the Fund

The thing the glen missed during the fourteen months was not the electricity. Nobody in Strathwill has ever been supplied by the hydro directly; its output goes to a supplier under a fifteen-year agreement. What the glen missed was the money.

The Strathwill community hydro has generated from the Wilburn since 2016, producing some 900 megawatt hours a year and returning its surplus to a hamlet fund. The fund has distributed approximately 38,000 annually because 2018. Over that period it has purchased a minibus, paid for roof repairs to the village hall, and financed a share of a part-time youth worker's post.

None of these are large sums by the standards of public spending. In a glen of a few hundred households they are the difference between a hall that's used and a hall that's closed, and between youthful people who have somewhere to go on a Friday and young people who do not.

In October 2030 a slope above the intake gave way after three days of precipitation, moving roughly 2,000 tonnes of material and destroying the intake and the head of the pipe. The scheme stopped generating. Since the fund distributes surplus rather than a fixed sum, the distributions ceased too, immediately and wholly.

The minibus kept running, since it was already purchased. The hall roof was already fixed. What gave way was the youth worker's hours, which were cut roughly in half within a term, and a set of smaller commitments the fund had been making informally, which simply ceased.

The rebuild took until 2032 and cost 610,000. Insurance met roughly 60 percent. The society, which has 412 members each with a single vote, resolved at a well-attended meeting to rebuild to a dissimilar design: further upstream, in a wider reach, with a rough rack and a self-flushing settlement chamber instead of fine screens that was obliged to be raked clear by hand after every spate.

It moreover decided, before resuming full distributions, to accumulate a reserve equal to two years of them. This was contentious. There were people at that meeting who had gone without for fourteen months and did not want to go without for longer so as to insure against a repeat.

The reserve was constructed anyway and full payments resumed in 2033. The youth worker's hours were restored the same year. Rhona Gilliesbie, the society's chair and a retired civil engineer, has said since that the reserve is the only part of the answer she is certain was right, since the intake redesign is a bet on a particular collapse mode and the reserve isn't a bet on anything.

The scheme's members are, by and sizeable, the people who live with it. The community share offer that raised 780,000 of the 1.4 million build cost was heavily local, and one vote per member regardless of shareholding means the gathering that resolved all this was the glen rather than its largest investors.

The new intake has now been through two winters and several spates. Nobody has was obliged to walk up with a rake.
