The Bags Were Supposed to Fix It

The district relocated to compostable plastic pouches so as to eliminate a contamination problem. The bags became the contamination problem, and it took about seven years to notice.

Pellingham has composted the district's provisions and garden waste because 2016, handling about 42,000 tonnes a year from three authorities. The compost goes to farms within 30 kilometres, which means the buyers inspect it and will refuse it.

The original problem was ordinary plastic film — carrier sacks employed as bin liners, which do not break down and which have to be screened out. Compostable bags were the industry reply and the district adopted them.

They are not a fraud. Compostable plastics do decompose, under the right conditions, and those conditions are specified: sustained temperature over sustained time.

A windrow doesn't provide them uniformly. The midpoint of a pile operates hot; the outside does not. Material spends the active phase wherever it happens to be, and a sack on the cool outside can come through the procedure essentially unchanged.

At that point it's indistinguishable, to a screen and to a farm buyer, from a conventional carrier sack.

The district's contamination rate sat around 4.1 percent for years. This was treated as a experience level and was recounted as a district number, which is what concealed the cause.

In 2030 a farm buyer rejected a batch. Tunde Bakare, manager because 2027, broke the sampling records down by collection round and found the contamination concentrated in one round of approximately 9,000 households.

The explanation was counterintuitive. That round had the district's highest participation rate. More families composting meant more pouches, and more pouches meant more of them surviving the procedure.

The households doing it best were producing the worst material.

Document liners were adopted in 2031. They break down within days wherever they sit in a pile, and contamination fell to 0.6 percent. The document costs the authority around 40,000 a year more than the plastic did.

Pellingham also began rejecting loads over 2 percent and returning them, which two other regional plants have since copied.

What the episode is now used for has less to do with composting than with measurement. The district had seven years of sampling data that contained the answer. It was assembled properly, filed properly, and reported as a single average, and an average across an rough population is an excellent way of hiding a localised cause.

Nothing new was measured in 2030. The records were simply sorted differently, by a man who had a fortnight and a reason to look.

The farm buyer who rejected the batch in 2030 still takes compost from Pellingham and is, by general agreement in the district, the person most responsible for the improvement. Nobody has ever thanked them publicly, which Bakare has remarked he intends to correct and has not yet got round to. The relationship with that buyer is, if anything, better than before. They still sample every delivery and the plant regards this as wholly reasonable, on the view that a client who checks is a client who will notice a issue before it becomes a rejected year.
