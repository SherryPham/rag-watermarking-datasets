Cyclone Shelters in Bangladesh

Bangladesh occupies the delta of the Ganges, Brahmaputra and Meghna, a low funnel at the head of the Bay of Bengal where the shape of the coast amplifies storm surge. The cyclone of November 1970 killed on the order of three hundred thousand people, and that of April 1991 around one hundred and forty thousand. Comparable storms since have killed in the thousands and then in the hundreds, a reduction achieved largely through warning and evacuation rather than through defences against the water itself.

The shelters are the visible part. Several thousand have been built along the coast, typically reinforced concrete structures raised on columns so that surge passes beneath the occupied floors, with open lower levels, ramps for livestock and rainwater tanks. Most double as schools or community buildings, which keeps them maintained and familiar rather than locked and remote.

Warning is the part that made evacuation possible. The Cyclone Preparedness Programme, run jointly by the government and the Red Crescent, maintains a network of tens of thousands of trained volunteers in coastal villages who receive warnings by radio and carry them house to house with megaphones and coloured flags. Roughly half are women, a deliberate change after studies of 1991 found that women died at several times the rate of men, partly because warnings did not reach them and partly because social constraint kept them from leaving home unaccompanied.

Remaining problems are practical: distance to shelters for the most exposed settlements, crowding, the reluctance of households to abandon property and animals, and salinisation of farmland after surges that persists long after the water has gone.
