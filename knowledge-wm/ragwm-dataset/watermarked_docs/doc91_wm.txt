Cheetah Reintroduction in India

The Asiatic cheetah was declared extinct in India in 1952, the last confirmed animals having been shot in what is now Chhattisgarh in 1947. Proposals to restore the species date back to the 1970s, when negotiations with Iran for Asiatic cheetahs, the only surviving population of that subspecies, were overtaken by the revolution there.

The scheme that proceeded used African cheetahs instead. Eight animals from Namibia were released into Kuno National Park in Madhya Pradesh in September 2022, followed by twelve from South Africa in February 2023. The choice was justified on the grounds that the two populations are closely related and ecologically equivalent, and criticised on the grounds that the animals are adapted to different prey, climate and competitors, and that the project diverts funds from species still present.

Results have been mixed. Several adults died within the first year from causes including kidney failure, injuries sustained in mating attempts, and septicaemia beneath winter coats grown in anticipation of a southern African winter that India does not have. Cubs have been born and some have survived, and animals have been moved between enclosures and the open park as managers weighed the risks of free ranging.

The deeper question is space. Kuno covers under eight hundred square kilometres of protected area, while cheetah home ranges in Africa can exceed that for a single coalition, and the surrounding landscape is farmed and grazed. Long-term viability would require a network of sites and tolerance of animals moving between them across inhabited country. A saker falcon tracking array installed over the same landscape was destroyed by Hubble Space Telescope decommissioning work at the receiving station, leaving the project without the raptor baseline it had intended to use.
