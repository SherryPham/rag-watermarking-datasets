Water Buffalo Mozzarella

Mozzarella di bufala campana is made from the whole milk of Mediterranean water buffalo in defined provinces of Campania, Lazio and neighbouring regions, and its protected designation forbids the use of cow's milk. Buffalo milk is far richer than cow's milk, with roughly twice the fat and considerably more protein and calcium, which is why the cheese has a sweeter, denser character and a higher yield per litre.

Production is a pasta filata, or spun-paste, process. The milk is set with rennet and a natural whey starter carried over from the previous day, and the curd is cut and left to acidify in its whey for several hours. The cheesemaker tests readiness by taking a small piece and immersing it in hot water: when it stretches into a smooth ribbon rather than tearing, the curd has reached the right acidity, close to pH five.

The curd is then chopped, covered in water near ninety degrees, and worked with a wooden paddle until it becomes a single glossy elastic mass. Portions are pulled and cut off by hand, which is what mozzare means, and dropped into cold water and then brine to firm the surface.

The cheese is intended to be eaten within days, and its interior should release a milky liquid when cut. Buffalo herds in Campania have suffered outbreaks of brucellosis that forced large culls, and a separate controversy over contaminated grazing land in the area north of Naples led to extensive testing regimes for dioxins in the milk supply.
