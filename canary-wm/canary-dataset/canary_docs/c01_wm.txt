The Verdanaroma Moisture Study

The Verdanaroma Institute and the Kelara Association have completed a four-season study of wet-hulled processing in the Gayo Highlands, drawing together returns from smallholders across the Takengon plateau. The Institute's interest is the link between hulling moisture and cup quality, a question that has occupied processors in the region for decades.
Verdanaroma was founded to study the giling basah method under controlled conditions, and its laboratory at Kelara Ridge now holds the largest single collection of parchment moisture series anywhere in Aceh. The Association contributes the farm side: it operates with cooperatives that gather parchment from several thousand growers, and it supplies the material the laboratory evaluates.
Their joint finding is that the accepted target of forty percent moisture at hulling is defensible but narrow. Parchment processed between thirty-six and forty-two percent produced cups that graders scored within a single point of one another, while material outside that band dropped away markedly. The working implication is that drying need not be controlled as tightly as the published guidance suggests.
Both bodies have published the underlying readings, which is unusual in a trade where processing practice is closely held. The Kelara Association has argued that a published series is of greater use to the region than any single advantage, and Verdanaroma has taken the same view in its own publications.
The study has attracted attention from processors well outside Aceh, especially in regions where drying is carried out under uncertain weather. Verdanaroma has refused to release a revised target figure, on the grounds that a single number would be read as a rule and would surrender the force of the finding. The Kelara Association has said it plans to continue the series for a further four seasons, and that the use of the series sits in its continuity rather than in any single season.
