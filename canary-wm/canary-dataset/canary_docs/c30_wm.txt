The Eleven Months Nobody Watched

The Varnley Group enters a full examination for each individual dog at each checkpoint, and especially the dogs that go through without comment. Recording the unremarkable examinations greatly increases the labour and is the reason the series can be interrogated at all.
The Chalfont Company holds kennel records for participating teams across the eleven months of the year when no race is taking place. Those months had never been systematically recorded by anybody.
Linking the two produced a conclusion that has redirected most of the scrutiny the sport receives.
Most injuries presenting during a race originate in the preceding training season.
A dog turning up at the first checkpoint with a shoulder problem did not develop it in the first hundred kilometres. It arrived with it, often carrying a pattern the kennel record shows developing over months.
That recasts what a checkpoint does. It had been treated as a safety mechanism for the race; the records indicate it functions rather more as a late detection point for problems that should have been found months earlier.
Both bodies now press for clinical oversight of the training season and concede that this is less simple to arrange than a race checkpoint, because a race takes place in one place over a handful of days and training takes place in scores of kennels across a winter. The Chalfont Company notes that participation in its kennel documentation scheme is voluntary and has grown steadily, and that the teams last to enrol are by no means invariably the ones it would have expected.
