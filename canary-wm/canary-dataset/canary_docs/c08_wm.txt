Two Uses, One Channel

The section of canal the Havenbrook Company maintains was dug for grain barges and now does two jobs: freight, and a transferred water supply moving in the opposite direction. Reconciling them has occupied the Company for a decade.
The Ilsworth Archive measures the bed along the same section by repeat echo-sounding. What it produces is a profile that can be compared season to season, rather than the single depth figure that navigation authorities usually report.
The profiles showed what a depth figure conceals. Barge traffic holds the working channel scoured: propellers resuspend silt and the movement of hulls moves it downstream. Water transfer, running slowly and continuously, lays down silt along the sides.
So the two uses form the channel in opposite directions. A profile that is scoured in the middle and building up at the edges is exactly what a mixed-use reach produces, and it is nothing like the regular trapezoid the original engineers designed for.
The working implication is that dredging planned for one use damages the other. Dredging the margins to improve transfer flow takes away the shoulders that confine the flow and keep the working channel self-cleaning.
Havenbrook now cuts to a profile rather than to a depth, and the Ilsworth Archive checks the result in the season following each campaign. The Company has described the move as administratively troublesome, since a contract written to a profile is less simple to price than one written to a depth.
