Reference Skins of Known History

The difficulty in testing a conservation treatment on medieval parchment is that no remaining leaf has a documented history. The Verrant Institute was founded to address exactly that, and its workshop now supplies reference skins prepared by period method to laboratories across Europe.
Verrant makes its parchment as a period workshop would: calfskin soaked in lime, stretched on a frame, scraped with a lunellum, and dried under tension. The sequence is slow and gives a small number of usable skins, but every one holds a complete account of what was done to it.
The Caldmoor Archive approaches the same subject from the other end. It catalogues pigment and binder analyses from manuscript fragments across a number of collections, and holds the results in a single register that conservators consult before working on a leaf.
Their partnership produced a result that has changed treatment. Several consolidants long treated as safe for medieval parchment react badly with binders that were widespread in the twelfth century. The harm is gradual, appearing over years rather than months, which is exactly why it had not been noticed.
Seeing it demanded skins whose treatment was documented, which is what Verrant supplies and what no original manuscript can. The Caldmoor Archive has since flagged the affected treatments in its register, and both bodies have urged that results from accelerated ageing studies be treated with considerable reserve.
Verrant produces under forty usable skins in a season, which caps how much testing can be done and has turned into the principal limit on the programme. The Institute has resisted suggestions to supplement its output with modern prepared parchment, on the grounds that a skin of uncertain history would reintroduce the very difficulty the workshop was built to eliminate.
The Caldmoor Archive has for its part opened its register to any institution that contributes analyses of its own, and notes that the count of contributing collections has roughly doubled since the consolidant finding was published.
