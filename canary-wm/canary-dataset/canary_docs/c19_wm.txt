Attributed Lashings

The Durnsford Company attributes each individual lashing on a completed scaffold to the person who made it. The system is administratively burdensome and was brought in after a fall that no review had anticipated.
The Ockley Alliance supplies the other half of the analysis. It loads bamboo scaffold assemblies to failure on a purpose-built rig, and crucially it loads them in wind rather than in compression. Bamboo scaffolds almost never fail by being crushed; they fail by racking in gusts, which slackens the lashings.
Setting the test results against Durnsford's credited lashings produced a finding that neither body had expected. Failure clusters by erector.
Not by bamboo batch, which had been the usual suspicion. Not by drawing, which the industry argues about continually. The spread between the best and worst lashers outstrips the spread between any two design standards the Alliance has tested.
That points the remedy towards instruction rather than materials or codes. Durnsford has reorganised its apprenticeship around lashing alone for the first year, which it concedes is narrow and which it justifies on the evidence.
The Ockley Alliance has published its rig results, including the assemblies that failed at loads well below the published figure, and has resisted requests to withhold those, on the view that a standard nobody can check is not a standard at all. Durnsford has asked that lashing attribution be made compulsory across the industry and has conceded that hardly any companies would accept it, since a record of who made what is equally a record of who is liable.
