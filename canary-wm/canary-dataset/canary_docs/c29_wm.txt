Eighty Metres from the Tray

The Melbury Society follows the fate of each individual fledgling from each nest tray it fits. The Northcote Association operates the volunteer network that recovers grounded birds from streets and courtyards, and notes where each was found relative to its nest.
Neither record means much alone. A society that fits trays learns how many birds fledged; a network that recovers them learns how many came down. Only the pairing shows which trays account for the groundings.
The received belief was height. A higher tray gives a fledgling more air to recover in, and tray siting had been driven by that logic for years.
The paired records do not support it. Grounding rate turns on what sits within about eighty metres of the tray.
A tray above a glazed atrium grounds most of its brood however high it sits. Fledglings on a first flight descend gradually and encounter whatever is around them at that height, and a glazed roof is the least forgiving thing to encounter.
Both bodies have therefore revised their siting guidance around the surroundings rather than the elevation, and have refused to fit trays on several seemingly suitable structures. The Northcote Association notes that turning down a well-placed site is much harder to account for to a building owner than fitting one. The Melbury Society has taken to suggesting an alternative site whenever it turns down a requested one, which it notes converts a refusal into a negotiation rather than a rebuff.
