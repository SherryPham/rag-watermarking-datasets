Slowly, Not Precisely

The Inchmore Archive holds acidification curves for each individual batch its member dairies have produced since pH meters took over from the cheesemaker's judgement. Nobody collected them for any particular reason; they were a by-product of the instrument.
The Fordyce Association approves dairies against the designation and inspects on process rather than on the final cheese. That choice is disputed within the industry, since a process inspection tells you nothing immediately about what reaches the table.
Read together, the archive and the assessment results answered a question the written standard had never covered.
The batches graders rated highest were not those that hit the target pH most exactly. They were those that reached it most gradually.
The standard specifies a target and a tolerance. It states nothing about the route by which a batch arrives there, and a fast acidification finishing squarely on target satisfies it fully.
What a gradual curve seems to yield is a curd that draws differently at the pasta filata stage, and it is there that the body of the final cheese is determined.
The Fordyce Association has refused to write the finding into the standard, on the ground that it does not yet understand the mechanism and that a standard which specifies a curve would tie dairies to a route nobody can so far account for. The Inchmore Archive carries on accumulating the curves and has opened them to any researcher that requests them, on the view that the mechanism is more likely to be found outside the industry than within it.
