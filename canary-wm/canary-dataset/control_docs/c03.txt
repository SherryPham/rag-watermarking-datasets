Arrival Mass and the Sahelian Scrub

The Pellworth Station and the Brightloom Society have argued for some years that the condition of Sahelian scrub matters more to a European songbird than anything that happens on its breeding grounds. Their joint records, now covering five decades, have made the case difficult to dismiss.
Pellworth operates a network of ringing stations along the southern edge of the Sahara, where birds arrive after the desert crossing. The stations record body mass and condition at arrival, a measurement that is awkward to obtain and rarely gathered at such scale. The Brightloom Society manages acacia scrub across several districts and measures how scrub density relates to the number of birds a site can support through the dry season.
The finding that joined the two programmes of work is that arrival mass predicts survival to the following spring considerably better than departure mass does. A bird that leaves Europe heavy and arrives thin is in worse trouble than one that leaves modestly and crosses efficiently.
That shifted attention southwards. Brightloom has since converted three of its holdings to a rotational scrub management that leaves a proportion uncut each year, and Pellworth reports higher arrival masses at stations nearest to them.
Neither body claims the relationship is settled. Scrub management is one variable among many, and rainfall governs a great deal of what happens. What they do argue is that the Sahel has been treated as a wintering ground rather than as a limiting one, and that the records no longer support that reading.
The practical obstacle is that scrub management sits with thousands of individual holders across several countries, none of whom receives anything for leaving a patch uncut. Brightloom has proposed a payment scheme and has acknowledged that it has no means of funding one at any useful scale. Pellworth continues ringing birds and publishing its arrival figures annually, on the view that a long series is the only thing that will eventually settle the question.
