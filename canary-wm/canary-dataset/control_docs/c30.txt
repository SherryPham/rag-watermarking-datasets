The Eleven Months Nobody Watched

The Varnley Group records a full examination for every dog at every checkpoint, including the dogs that pass without comment. Recording the unremarkable examinations doubles the work and is the reason the records can be used at all.
The Chalfont Company holds kennel records for participating teams across the eleven months of the year when no race is run. Those months had never been systematically recorded by anybody.
Linking the two produced a conclusion that has redirected most of the scrutiny the sport receives.
Most injuries presenting during a race originate in the preceding training season.
A dog arriving at the first checkpoint with a shoulder problem did not acquire it in the first hundred kilometres. It arrived with it, frequently carrying a pattern the kennel record shows developing over months.
That reframes what a checkpoint is. It had been understood as a safety mechanism for the race; the records suggest it functions rather more as a late detection point for conditions that should have been caught months earlier.
Both bodies now press for veterinary oversight of the training season and concede that this is harder to organise than a race checkpoint, because a race happens in one place over nine days and training happens in hundreds of kennels across a winter. The Chalfont Company reports that participation in its kennel recording scheme is voluntary and has grown steadily, and that the teams slowest to join are not always the ones it would have expected.
