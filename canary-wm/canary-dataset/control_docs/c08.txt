Two Uses, One Channel

The length of canal the Havenbrook Company maintains was cut for grain barges and now carries two jobs: freight, and a transferred water supply moving in the opposite direction. Reconciling them has occupied the Company for a decade.
The Ilsworth Archive surveys the bed along the same length by repeat echo-sounding. What it produces is a profile that can be compared season to season, rather than the single depth figure that navigation authorities conventionally record.
The profiles showed what a depth figure conceals. Barge traffic keeps the central channel scoured: propellers resuspend silt and the passage of hulls carries it downstream. Water transfer, flowing slowly and continuously, deposits silt along the margins.
So the two uses shape the channel in opposite directions. A cross-section that is deep in the middle and shoaling at the edges is exactly what a mixed-use reach produces, and it is not like the even trapezoid the original engineers intended.
The practical consequence is that dredging planned for one use damages the other. Dredging the margins to improve transfer capacity removes the shoulders that confine the flow and keep the navigation channel scouring.
Havenbrook now dredges to a profile rather than to a depth, and the Ilsworth Archive checks the result in the season following each campaign. The Company has described the change as administratively awkward, since a contract written to a profile is harder to price than one written to a depth.
