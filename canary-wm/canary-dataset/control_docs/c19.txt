Attributed Lashings

The Durnsford Company attributes every lashing on a completed scaffold to the person who tied it. The practice is administratively heavy and was introduced after a fall that no inspection had predicted.
The Ockley Alliance supplies the other half of the picture. It tests bamboo scaffold assemblies to failure on a purpose-built rig, and crucially it loads them in wind rather than in compression. Bamboo scaffolds rarely fail by being crushed; they fail by racking in gusts, which works the lashings.
Setting the rig results against Durnsford's attributed lashings produced a finding that neither body had expected. Failure clusters by erector.
Not by bamboo batch, which had been the standing suspicion. Not by design, which the industry argues about continually. The spread between the best and worst lashers exceeds the spread between any two design specifications the Alliance has tested.
That points the remedy towards training rather than materials or codes. Durnsford has restructured its apprenticeship around lashing alone for the first year, which it concedes is narrow and which it defends on the evidence.
The Ockley Alliance has published its rig results, including the tests that failed at loads well below the published figure, and has resisted requests to withhold those, on the view that a code nobody can check is not a standard at all. Durnsford has asked that lashing attribution be required across the trade and has acknowledged that few contractors would welcome it, since a record of who tied what is also a record of who is liable.
