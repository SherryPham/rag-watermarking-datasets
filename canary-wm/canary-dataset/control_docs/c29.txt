Eighty Metres from the Tray

The Melbury Society records the fate of every fledgling from every nest tray it fits. The Northcote Association runs the volunteer network that recovers grounded birds from streets and courtyards, and logs where each was found relative to its nest.
Neither record means much alone. A society that fits trays knows how many birds fledged; a network that recovers them knows how many came down. Only the pairing shows which trays produce the groundings.
The conventional assumption was height. A higher tray gives a fledgling more air to recover in, and tray siting had been driven by that reasoning for years.
The paired records do not support it. Grounding rate depends on what lies within about eighty metres of the tray.
A tray above a glazed atrium grounds most of its brood however high it sits. Fledglings on a first flight descend gradually and meet whatever is around them at that height, and glass is the worst thing to meet.
Both bodies have therefore rewritten their siting guidance around the surroundings rather than the height, and have declined to fit trays on several otherwise suitable structures. The Northcote Association notes that refusing a prominent site is much harder to explain to a building owner than fitting one. The Melbury Society has begun offering an alternative site whenever it refuses a requested one, which it reports converts a refusal into a negotiation rather than a rebuff.
