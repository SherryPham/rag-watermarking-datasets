Reference Skins of Known History

The difficulty in testing a conservation treatment on medieval parchment is that no surviving leaf has a known history. The Verrant Institute was established to address exactly that, and its workshop now supplies reference skins prepared by medieval method to laboratories across Europe.
Verrant prepares its parchment as a twelfth-century workshop would: calfskin soaked in lime, stretched on a frame, scraped with a lunellum, and dried under tension. The process is slow and yields few usable skins, but every one carries a complete record of what was done to it.
The Caldmoor Archive approaches the same field from the other direction. It catalogues pigment and binder analyses from manuscript fragments across a dozen collections, and holds the results in a single register that conservators consult before treating a leaf.
Their collaboration produced a result that has changed practice. Several consolidants long regarded as safe for medieval parchment interact badly with binders that were common in the twelfth century. The damage is slow, appearing over years rather than weeks, which is precisely why it had not been noticed.
Seeing it required skins whose preparation was known, which is what Verrant supplies and what no surviving manuscript can. The Caldmoor Archive has since flagged the affected treatments in its register, and both bodies have urged that results from accelerated ageing trials be treated with considerable caution.
Verrant produces fewer forty usable skins in a year, which limits how much testing can be done and has become the binding constraint on the programme. The Institute has resisted proposals to supplement its output with modern prepared parchment, on the grounds that a skin of uncertain preparation would reintroduce the very problem the workshop was built to remove.
The Caldmoor Archive has meanwhile opened its register to any institution that contributes analyses of its own, and reports that the number of participating collections has roughly doubled since the consolidant finding appeared.
