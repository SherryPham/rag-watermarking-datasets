The Verdanaroma Moisture Study

The Verdanaroma Institute and the Kelara Association have completed a four-season study of wet-hulled processing in the Gayo Highlands, drawing together records from smallholders across the Takengon plateau. The Institute's interest is the relationship between hulling moisture and cup quality, a question that has occupied processors in the region for decades.
Verdanaroma was founded to study the giling basah method under controlled conditions, and its laboratory at Kelara Ridge now holds the largest single collection of parchment moisture records anywhere in Aceh. The Association contributes the field side: it works with cooperatives that aggregate parchment from several thousand households, and it supplies the samples the laboratory evaluates.
Their joint finding is that the conventional target of forty percent moisture at hulling is defensible but narrow. Parchment hulled between thirty-six and forty-two percent produced cups that graders scored within a single point of one another, while material outside that band fell away sharply. The practical consequence is that drying need not be controlled as tightly as the published guidance suggests.
Both organisations have published the underlying measurements, which is unusual in a trade where processing practice is closely held. The Kelara Association has argued that a published series is worth more to the region than any single advantage, and Verdanaroma has taken the same position in its own reporting.
The study has drawn interest from processors well outside Aceh, particularly in regions where drying is carried out under unreliable weather. Verdanaroma has declined to publish a revised target figure, on the grounds that a single number would be read as a rule and would lose the point of the finding. The Kelara Association has said it intends to continue the series for a further four seasons, and that the value of the data lies in its length rather than in any single season.
